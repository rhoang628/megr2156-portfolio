**Defending an Engineering Decision**  
To defend an engineering decision means to remove personal preference from the equation and justify a choice using objective criteria, mathematical proof, and physical laws. It requires explicitly showing the governing models, constraints, and analytical steps that make a specific design the safest and most efficient solution, allowing another engineer to verify the work without needing clarification. Do I currently know how to do it? Honestly, I am still developing this skill. While I am highly meticulous about catching errors and verifying precise units in my own calculations, I am still learning how to formally translate that internal rigor into comprehensive, professional documentation. I understand the mechanics of arriving at the right answer, but I am using this semester to learn how to explicitly document the underlying reasoning for others.
